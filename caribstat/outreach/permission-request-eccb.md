# Draft: permission request to ECCB

To: info@eccb-centralbank.org
Subject: Permission request. Citing ECCB statistics through an automated, attributed data service

Dear ECCB Statistics Department,

I maintain StatCite (https://statcite.com), a free service that gives AI assistants and researchers direct, citable access to official economic statistics. Every figure it serves carries a full citation back to the original source. Publisher, dataset, series, exact URL, and retrieval date. Specifically so users go to the primary source rather than trusting an AI's memory of a number.

I'd like to add the ECCB's published statistics (the Summarized Monetary Survey, CPI, tourism, fiscal accounts, and public sector debt tables on eccb-centralbank.org/statistics) to this service, refreshed on a regular schedule, with the ECCB credited as the source and a direct link to the relevant ECCB table on every value served.

I read your Disclaimer Policy, which grants use of the site for personal, noncommercial purposes without a right to redistribute or compile the material. I'm writing before building anything further because what I'm describing goes beyond that grant, and I'd rather ask than assume.

Specifically, I'm requesting permission to:
- Fetch your published statistics tables on a scheduled basis (weekly or per your preferred cadence)
- Store and serve the values through StatCite's API, each one attributed to the ECCB with a link to the source table and the "data as at" date your site already publishes
- Make this available to AI agents and researchers, currently free of charge

I'm glad to follow any conditions you'd set. A specific attribution format, a request rate limit, a designated contact for questions, or anything else that works on your end. I can also send a sample of exactly what a served value and its citation look like if that's useful for your review.

Thank you for considering this, and for publishing the data in the first place. It's a genuine gap that regional statistics aren't available anywhere agents can use them, and the ECCB is the obvious authoritative source to fix that.

Best regards,
